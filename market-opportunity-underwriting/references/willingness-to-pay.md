# Pricing and Willingness to Pay

## Evidence order

Prefer:

1. actual paid prices and contracts;
2. observed substitute/workaround spending;
3. real price or packaging experiments;
4. procurement/budget evidence;
5. structured choice or reservation-price research with real respondents;
6. interviews/surveys;
7. attention or model-generated opinions.

Do not equate enthusiasm with WTP.

## Price bounds

When possible record:

- **floor:** lowest price consistent with the business model / positioning;
- **base:** best-supported price or range;
- **ceiling:** highest plausible price given value, alternatives, budget, and evidence.

Each bound needs an epistemic state.

## Discrete choice / conjoint

Discrete-choice and conjoint methods can estimate trade-offs and preference structures when the experimental design and respondent data are real and appropriate. They are not desk-research magic: without respondent/behavioral data, recommend the experiment rather than simulate a result.
