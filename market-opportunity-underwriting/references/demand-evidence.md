# Demand Evidence

## Principle

A gap is not a market. And "demand" is not one variable.

Keep at least four judgments separate:

1. **Problem economic burden** — is the problem consequential enough that buyers/users already sacrifice money, labor, risk, delay, or opportunity?
2. **Budget availability** — is there an identifiable buyer, budget source, procurement path, or reallocatable spend?
3. **Solution willingness to pay** — is there evidence that buyers will transfer budget to this proposed value proposition at a realistic price?
4. **Behavioral adoption** — is there evidence that users will switch, implement, repeatedly use, retain, or otherwise change behavior?

Reachability is assessed separately in the acquisition/adoption model.

A company can have a severe economic problem with weak solution WTP. It can have strong WTP with difficult adoption. Do not collapse those differences into one "demand score."

## Evidence proximity / type

| Tier | Evidence | What it can support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | purchases, contracts, paid pilots, deposits, switching, budget reallocation | strongest direct evidence for WTP/budget and sometimes adoption |
| B | substitute spend, consultants, internal labor, workaround stacks, measurable problem cost | strong economic-burden evidence; not automatically solution WTP |
| C | repeated usage, retention, procurement, internal-build attempts, high-intensity workarounds | behavioral adoption/importance, depending on context |
| D | choice-based conjoint, discrete choice, real pricing experiments, reservation-price studies | structured WTP/preference evidence when based on real respondents/behavior |
| E | surveys, interviews, stated purchase intent | useful context, vulnerable to hypothetical bias |
| F | search, social discussion, traffic, press, community activity | attention/discovery signal only |

Tier E/F evidence must never be promoted into "market demonstrated" without stronger support.

## Questions to answer

1. What do customers do today?
2. What money, labor, risk, delay, or opportunity cost do they incur?
3. Who controls the relevant budget and where does it come from?
4. What evidence shows the problem is consequential rather than merely annoying?
5. What evidence supports willingness to transfer money or budget to this solution at the proposed price?
6. What evidence supports actual switching, implementation, repeated use, or retention?
7. What evidence would show that economic burden does **not** translate into WTP?

## AI evidence limitation

Synthetic personas, AI role-play, generated survey respondents, or model-predicted preferences are not customer evidence. AI may analyze real evidence but does not become the sample.
