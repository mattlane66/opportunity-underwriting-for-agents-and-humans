# Institutional Investor Scrutiny

## Purpose

The underwriting standard should become stricter as real operating evidence becomes available. Rigor means **using the strongest evidence appropriate to the stage**, not demanding late-stage metrics from a company that does not yet have them.

This module governs the commercial/market portion of diligence. It does not replace accounting, legal, tax, technical, financing, management, cyber/security, or other transaction diligence.

## Scrutiny profiles

### General

Use the core protocol. Make the decision hurdle explicit and expose every load-bearing assumption/unknown.

### Venture — seed

Primary questions:

- Is the problem economically consequential?
- Is there credible evidence of budget or willingness to pay?
- Is a sufficiently large market plausible from bottom-up bounds?
- Why now?
- Is there at least one plausible distribution path?
- What is the fatal unknown that should be tested before meaningful capital commitment?

Expected evidence can include current economic sacrifice, paid pilots/deposits where available, strong behavioral workarounds, real buyer budgets, credible bottom-up denominators, and early pricing tests.

Do **not** require observed CAC, retention, or mature cohorts when they do not yet exist.

### Venture — early

Add scrutiny on:

- repeatability of the buyer/use case;
- pipeline quality versus founder-network one-offs;
- sales cycle and implementation effort;
- actual price/ACV;
- activation/retention where observable;
- gross margin and early unit-economic direction;
- channel repeatability.

Distinguish initial burst revenue from a repeatable sales motion.

### Venture — growth

Expect standardized, cohort-based operating evidence:

- gross and net retention;
- revenue growth by segment/cohort;
- gross margin;
- CAC/payback using a consistent definition;
- sales efficiency;
- concentration;
- expansion versus new-logo growth;
- market penetration and remaining runway;
- channel saturation and marginal acquisition efficiency.

Benchmark definitions must be normalized before comparison.

### Growth equity

In addition to growth-stage evidence, emphasize:

- durability of growth;
- incremental/marginal unit economics, not only historical averages;
- segment economics;
- customer concentration and churn exposure;
- pricing power;
- path to cash generation;
- addressable runway at the scale required by the valuation;
- downside if growth normalizes faster than management expects.

### Private equity / acquisition — commercial diligence

Treat the subject as a business with operating evidence, not a startup narrative.

Commercial underwriting should scrutinize:

- market size, growth, cyclicality, and structural drivers;
- market share and share movement;
- customer concentration and cohort retention;
- pricing, discounting, elasticity, and renewal behavior;
- win/loss and switching evidence;
- channel economics and dependence;
- competitive position / right to win;
- organic-growth quality;
- margin and cash-conversion implications of the commercial model;
- plausible value-creation initiatives and the evidence behind them;
- downside / cleansheet reunderwriting of the thesis;
- exit-buyer evidence needs when relevant.

A market-opportunity verdict is **not** a full acquisition recommendation. Explicitly list adjacent diligence still required.

### Corporate market entry

Emphasize:

- strategic fit;
- existing channel/customer leverage;
- capability gaps;
- cannibalization;
- build/buy/partner alternatives;
- required investment and hurdle;
- time to economic contribution;
- organizational constraints.

## Persisted scrutiny coverage

For any non-general scrutiny profile, the workspace creates a required commercial-review checklist. Each item must end in one of:

- `EVIDENCED` — supported by inspectable evidence;
- `UNKNOWN` — decision-relevant but not currently knowable from available evidence;
- `NOT_APPLICABLE` — genuinely irrelevant to this business/decision;
- `OUTSIDE_SCOPE` — belongs to an adjacent diligence lane rather than this commercial method.

Every non-`UNASSESSED` status requires reasoning; `EVIDENCED` should link evidence IDs. A final verdict cannot leave required scrutiny checks `UNASSESSED`.

This is deliberately stricter than forcing a numeric benchmark. The system must surface missing evidence rather than fabricate stage-appropriate-looking metrics.

## Investment-committee challenge standard

Before a final `PURSUE` recommendation, a skeptical reviewer should be able to answer:

1. What is the actual decision hurdle?
2. What 2–3 claims carry most of the thesis?
3. Which of those are observed versus assumed?
4. What evidence would make the thesis false?
5. Were contrary cases and failed analogs searched deliberately?
6. Are cited sources genuinely independent or repeats of one lineage?
7. Can every material market number be reproduced?
8. Does the conclusion survive plausible alternative market definitions?
9. Which parts of the thesis are not knowable without new empirical evidence?
10. What is the highest-value next evidence if the decision remains uncertain?

A polished narrative is not a substitute for satisfactory answers.
